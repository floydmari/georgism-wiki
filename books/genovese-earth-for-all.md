## Overview

*The Earth for All: Henry George, Utopian Communities and Market Socialism* (McFarland,
2025) is a book by **Jeremy E. C. Genovese**, emeritus associate professor of human
development and educational psychology at Cleveland State University. Genovese — himself a
former resident of intentional communities and a member of the Communal Studies
Association — examines historical attempts to build alternative societies organized on
shared principles and collective property ownership, asking what distinguished the
utopian experiments that persisted for decades or generations from the many that collapsed
within months. Among the most durable, the book argues, were communities inspired by [Henry
George](/wiki/henry-george/)'s vision of individual freedom coupled with economic justice.

## Scope and Structure

Per the publisher's table of contents, the book moves from general utopian-communities
theory ("Were Utopian Communities Failures?", "The Utopian Dilemma") through a survey of
religious communal experiments (the Shakers), Edward Bellamy's *Looking Backward* and the
real-world "Bellamy colonies" it inspired, then into its Georgist core: a chapter on "The
Message of Henry George," a chapter tracing **Marie Howland**'s transition "From Fourierism
to the Single Tax," and a chapter titled simply "The Georgist Utopias." Later chapters
("Utopia and Behaviorism," "Justice and Utopia") broaden into theoretical reflection, with
an appendix connecting Esperanto (a cause Genovese is separately active in, as Great Lakes
coordinator for Esperanto-USA) to market fundamentalism. The book's subject tags explicitly
name **Arden, Delaware** among its case studies — the single-tax colony the wiki already
documents in depth.

## Relation to the Wiki's Existing Coverage

This is the theoretical and comparative companion to material the wiki already covers from
the practical-history side: [Single-Tax Colonies](/wiki/single-tax-colonies/) documents
Fairhope and Arden as specific historical experiments, and the [Fairhope Single Tax
Corporation](/wiki/fairhope-single-tax-corporation/) page covers the still-operating
Alabama colony in institutional detail. Genovese's book situates those specific American
experiments within the broader 19th–20th century utopian-communities tradition (Bellamy
colonies, Marie Howland's Fourierist-to-Georgist arc, Shaker communities), asking a
comparative question — what made *any* utopian community durable — that neither of the
wiki's existing colony-specific pages addresses directly.
