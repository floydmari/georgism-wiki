## Overview

**Henry George Jr.** (November 3, 1862 – November 14, 1916) was the eldest son of economist [Henry George](/wiki/henry-george/) — newspaperman, two-term U.S. Congressman, and author of the standard filial biography of his father, as well as his own single-tax polemic. Born in Sacramento, California, he began work in a printing office at sixteen, moved with his family to Brooklyn in 1880, and worked as a *Brooklyn Eagle* reporter from 1881 before accompanying his father as secretary on George's 1884 lecture tour of Great Britain.[1][4]

## Early Career and Editorial Work

After the 1884 British tour, George Jr. joined the staff of the London *Truth*, then the *North American Review*, and served as managing editor of the Georgist weekly *The Standard* from 1887 to 1891. He went on to work as a Washington correspondent for a syndicate of Western newspapers (1891) and as that syndicate's London correspondent (1892), before becoming managing editor of the *Florida Citizen* in Jacksonville in 1893.[1][4] On December 2, 1897 — roughly five weeks after his father's death during the 1897 mayoral campaign — he married Marie Morelle Hitch of Orleans Parish, Louisiana.[4] [VERIFY: the marriage date and Hitch's biographical details are corroborated only by the English Wikipedia article in this session, not independently cross-checked against a primary genealogical or newspaper record.]

## Congressional Career

Elected as a Democrat from New York, George Jr. served two terms in the U.S. House of Representatives — the 62nd and 63rd Congresses, from March 4, 1911 to March 3, 1915. As a Congressman he gave speeches and sponsored bills favoring a single tax on land values.[1][2] He did not seek reelection in 1914 and afterward devoted himself to literary work until his death in Washington, D.C. in 1916; he is interred at Green-Wood Cemetery, Brooklyn — the same cemetery where his father and sister Jennie are buried.[1][2]

While in Congress, he made a publicized visit to [Vancouver](/wiki/vancouver/) in October 1911, at the height of that city's land-only municipal tax system, to promote his father's economic theories; he was reported as remarking that "Vancouver is regarded as an advanced city, as a city leading the way."[3]

## *The Menace of Privilege* (1905)

Between the biography of his father and his own run for Congress, George Jr. published ***The Menace of Privilege: A Study of the Dangers to the Republic from the Existence of a Favored Class*** (New York: The Macmillan Company, 1905; xii + 421 pp.) — his own extended single-tax argument, distinct from his work as his father's biographer.[5] The book opens by asking what is driving "the extraordinary inequality in the distribution of wealth," the "growth of the aristocratic idea," and the "corruption of Federal, State and municipal politics," and answers: "That something is Privilege" — a claim it develops across chapters titled *Land of Inequality*, *The Cause of Inequality*, *Types of Princes of Privilege*, *Aristocracy A Fruit of Privilege*, and *Despoilment of the Masses*, among others.[5] Its prescription follows his father's: "Tax land monopoly to death, thereby enabling the remission of all taxation now embarrassing production, and take all public highway functions into public hands" (Preface).[5] This is a D-claim as far as the wiki treats it evidentially — the book is a polemic making George Jr.'s own argument, not a disinterested study, and is cited here for what it shows about his own thought and public career rather than as independent support for single-tax claims elsewhere on the wiki. ## *Life of Henry George* (1900)

After his father's fatal 1897 mayoral campaign, George Jr. spent three years compiling family papers, letters, and his own memory into ***The Life of Henry George*** (Doubleday & McClure, 1900) — the standard primary biography, and the source nearly every later account of George's life builds on or argues with, including [Barker's 1955 academic biography](/wiki/barker-henry-george-biography/). The book is public domain and its complete text is hosted on this wiki; see [The Life of Henry George](/wiki/life-of-henry-george/) for the full work and [sources/publicdomain/life-of-henry-george.md](https://github.com/floydmari/georgism-wiki/blob/main/sources/publicdomain/life-of-henry-george.md) for the raw transcription. It is a sympathetic, filial account — documentary gold on dates and correspondence, but cited on this wiki with that loyalty noted (D-claims where the son characterizes his father).

## Nuances and Limits

- **Primary-source status, not independent secondary scholarship.** Wherever this wiki cites George Jr. for episodes in his father's life (the 1886 and 1897 mayoral campaigns, the Irish Land War reporting, the "flash of insight" account), the citation is to a family member's memoir, not a disinterested historian — treated as A-claims for quoted primary material (letters, speeches, vote totals) and D-claims for interpretation.
- **His own political career is comparatively thin in the record here.** Beyond the congressional basics and the Vancouver visit, this page does not yet cover his 1897 mayoral candidacy (he was put forward, with little support, to carry his father's ticket after George Sr.'s death) in depth — see [The Last Campaign, 1897](/wiki/1897-nyc-mayoral-campaign/). It also does not yet cover the reception, sales, or influence of *The Menace of Privilege* — see the `[CITATION NEEDED]` note above.
- **Do not conflate father and son.** *Progress and Poverty* (1879), the 1886 and 1897 mayoral campaigns, and the Irish Land War reporting are the father's; the congressional career, *The Menace of Privilege*, and the authorship (not the subject) of *Life of Henry George* are the son's. This page cites George Jr. only for what he himself did, wrote, or witnessed.
