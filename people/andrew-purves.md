## Overview

**Andrew Purves** is a London-based Georgist economist and author, affiliated with the [School
of Economic Science](/wiki/school-of-economic-science/) (now the School of Philosophy and
Economic Science) and an active contributor to UK land-value-tax policy work through the
[Coalition for Economic Justice](/wiki/coalition-for-economic-justice/) and direct submissions
to UK and Scottish government bodies.

## No Debt, High Growth, Low Tax (2015)

Purves is the author of *No Debt, High Growth, Low Tax: Hong Kong's Economic Miracle
Explained* (Shepheard-Walwyn, November 2015), which examines how [Hong Kong](/wiki/hong-kong/)
financed public transit and infrastructure by capturing land value directly — the government
leasing land at pre-development prices to the [MTR Corporation](/wiki/mtr-corporation/), which
then develops and retains the resulting real-estate value uplift around its own stations,
generating substantial ongoing profit (cited elsewhere as HK$10.3 billion, roughly £792 million,
in the year to March 2016) without public subsidy.[1][4] The book is cited repeatedly in his
later policy work as the model case for infrastructure-financed-by-land-value-capture.

## Land Value Tax: Overcoming the Challenges (2018)

Purves co-authored, with Stephen Stretton and Tony Vickers, the cover-story article "Land Value
Tax: Overcoming the Challenges" in *Land & Liberty* No. 1243 (Summer 2018), published by the
[Henry George Foundation (UK)](/wiki/henry-george-foundation-uk/).[2] The article was explicitly
part of a formal submission by the School of Economic Science, with the Foundation's support, to
the Scottish Land Commission's 2017 tender inviting research on international land-value-tax
experience to identify policy options for Scotland.[2] Beyond surveying LVT's history (from the
Physiocrats through Henry George, the UK's 1909 People's Budget, and repeated post-war British
betterment-charge attempts that were each repealed) and international comparisons (Denmark, the
Baltic states), the article proposes two original mechanisms: **Norm-Based Taxation**, designed
to capture LVT's full economic effect via indexed revaluation without an initial jump in
individual tax bills, and **Location Value Covenants (LVCs)**, a scheme — building on Robin
Wrigley's 2010/2017 proposal — to replace conventional mortgages with land sale-and-leaseback
contracts issued by a central bank or "Community Land Society."[2]

## UK Parliament Written Evidence (2018)

In March 2018, Purves and Dr Peter Bowman submitted formal written evidence — reference LVC0035,
explicitly "on behalf of the School of Economic Science" — to the UK Parliament Housing,
Communities and Local Government Committee's land value capture inquiry.[3] The submission
argued existing UK land-value-capture mechanisms are "partial, contested, complex, discretionary,
costly to negotiate," estimating that of £12.4 billion in land value uplift in England in
2014-15, only about £3.12 billion was captured through Section 106 agreements, Community
Infrastructure Levies, and public land sales — leaving roughly £9.3 billion uncaptured — and
again cited Hong Kong's MTR model as a comparison, alongside recommendations to build cross-party
support, consider gradual implementation via reformed Business Rates or Council Tax, and design
transition mechanisms to protect existing owners.[3]

## Coalition for Economic Justice

Purves joined the [Coalition for Economic Justice](/wiki/coalition-for-economic-justice/)'s
steering group in 2016-17, and is separately credited as a contributor to C4EJ's 2018-19 Working
Group submission on redeveloping London's Old Oak and Park Royal district (OPDC), alongside Ed
Randall and John Lipetz.[4] He also spoke at a C4EJ open meeting in June 2016 on Hong Kong
transit infrastructure financing.[5]
