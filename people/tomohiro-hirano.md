## Overview

**Tomohiro Hirano** is a Japanese macroeconomist, currently **Associate Professor of
Economics at [Royal Holloway, University of London](https://www.royalholloway.ac.uk/)**,
a **Research Associate at the Centre for Macroeconomics at the London School of
Economics**, and a **Research Fellow at the Canon Institute for Global Studies**
(Tokyo).[1][2][3] He holds a **PhD in Economics from the University of Tokyo**, where he
began his academic career, and his research specializes in **asset-price bubbles,
liquidity, and financial regulation** within formal overlapping-generations (OLG) macro
models.[4]

Before his sustained collaboration with [Joseph Stiglitz](/wiki/joseph-stiglitz/), Hirano
built his reputation with **Noriyuki Yanagawa** — most notably "Asset Bubbles, Endogenous
Growth, and Financial Frictions," published in the *Review of Economic Studies* 84(1)
(2017), a widely cited paper on how rational asset-price bubbles can coexist with, and
even support, productive investment under credit constraints — and with **Masaru Inaba**
and Yanagawa on "Asset Bubbles and Bailouts," *Journal of Monetary Economics* 76
(2015).[5][6] This earlier body of work on bubbles-and-growth interactions is the direct
methodological ancestor of the land-speculation research program he later built with
Stiglitz.

## The Hirano-Stiglitz Research Program (2022–2026)

Since at least 2022, Hirano has co-authored with Stiglitz a sustained, technically
demanding sequence of papers using OLG models to formalize how **land speculation, credit,
and monetary/fiscal policy** interact to shape long-run economic growth and macroeconomic
fluctuations — the most developed body of formal macroeconomic theory connecting land
markets to growth currently on this wiki. The program's papers, as covered here:

- [Land Speculation and Wobbly Dynamics with Endogenous Phase Transitions](/wiki/hirano-stiglitz-wobbly-dynamics-land-speculation/) (2022) — the foundational land-and-capital model of endogenous boom-bust land-price cycles.
- [The Wobbly Economy: Global Dynamics with Phase and State Transitions](/wiki/hirano-stiglitz-wobbly-economy-global-dynamics/) (2022) — the general (land-free) mathematical mechanism underlying the above.
- [Credit, Land Speculation, and Long-Run Economic Growth / Low-Interest-Rate Policy](/wiki/hirano-stiglitz-credit-land-speculation-growth/) (2024–2025) — sectoral credit composition as the key growth determinant; a land tax redirecting credit raises the growth rate.
- [Growth and Fluctuations in Economies with Land Speculation](/wiki/hirano-stiglitz-growth-fluctuations-land-speculation/) (2025) — land substantially widens the range of possible fluctuations; accommodative policy can backfire when credit constraints bind.
- [Overlapping Generations Models, Multiplicity of Steady States and Momentary Equilibria, and Economic Fluctuations](/wiki/hirano-stiglitz-olg-multiplicity-fluctuations/) (2025) — the general mathematical foundation proving multiplicity is a structural OLG possibility, not a land-specific artifact.
- [Henry George, Land Speculation, and Economic Growth and Transformation](/wiki/oxford-review-george-2025/) (2025) — the program's flagship, explicitly Georgist-titled paper, published in the *Oxford Review of Economic Policy*.

Read together, this is a coherent multi-year project: two general theoretical foundations
(the "wobbly economy" and OLG-multiplicity papers), two land-and-credit applications, and
a policy-facing synthesis that engages Henry George's argument directly. The wiki treats
this as its primary source base for formal, contemporary macroeconomic theory of land
speculation's effect on growth — distinct from, and considerably more mathematically
developed than, the wiki's more empirical/historical [18-Year Land
Cycle](/wiki/18-year-land-cycle/) coverage.

## Relation to the Georgist Case

Hirano is not a self-described Georgist and his earlier work (with Yanagawa and Inaba) is
general asset-bubble theory with no land-specific or Georgist framing at all. What makes
his collaboration with Stiglitz significant for this wiki is that it applies the same
rigorous OLG bubble-and-growth machinery specifically to **land**, and — in the flagship
2025 paper — explicitly to Henry George's own argument, arriving at conclusions
structurally sympathetic to the Georgist case (land speculation crowds out productive
investment; land taxation can raise the growth rate) while also surfacing genuine
complications a simple Georgist reading would miss (an isolated land tax can, in some of
the program's models, widen rather than narrow price-fluctuation ranges away from the
steady state). The wiki should present Hirano, like Stiglitz, as mainstream theoretical
validation with real nuance, not an uncomplicated endorsement.
