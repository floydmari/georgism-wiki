## Summary

"Contested rent-based urban governance: Land rent dissipation and redistribution under
scalar politics in China," by **Jiayao Liu and Jin Zhu**, appeared in *Land Use Policy* 157
(2025), October issue, as CC-BY open access. It examines a dimension of Chinese land-rent
politics distinct from the wiki's existing China cluster: not tax-policy design, not
state-versus-private-developer capture, but **contestation over land rent between different
tiers of government within the state itself**.

## The Zhongshan Case

Using mixed methods — interviews, archival research, and statistical analysis — the paper
traces a case in **Zhongshan** where **implicit land rent distribution prompted rent-seeking
by discretionary lower-level (sub-municipal/district) governments**, ultimately producing a
**developmental crisis and rent dissipation**. A subsequent **scalar restructuring**
reallocated authority between government tiers in response, but the paper finds these reform
efforts themselves "impaired by entrenched rent dissipation and the real estate downturn" —
the crisis the restructuring was meant to fix had already begun eroding the very land-rent
base any new intergovernmental arrangement would depend on.

## Relation to the Georgist Case

This is a genuinely distinct angle within the wiki's substantial existing China
cluster. [Xu's "active mode" land-finance paper](/wiki/xu-china-land-finance-active-mode/)
frames China as a public-authorities-versus-private-developers story (the state organizing
development and capturing land value directly, contrasted with the developer-led "passive
mode" typical of most developing countries). Liu & Zhu's contribution operates on an entirely
different axis: **within** the state itself, different government tiers compete over an
implicitly, rather than formally, allocated land-rent stream, and that internal contestation
— not developer capture — is what produces dissipation and crisis. It is a useful complication
for the wiki's [land value capture](/wiki/land-value-capture/) coverage generally: even where
land value is captured publicly rather than privately, *which* level of government captures
it, and how well-defined that allocation is, is itself a live governance problem with
real fiscal consequences.

## Nuances and Limits

- **A single Chinese city case study (Zhongshan).** The specific dynamic of implicit,
  contested inter-tier rent allocation may not generalize identically to other Chinese
  cities' land-finance systems, let alone other countries'.
- **Abstract-level source (B+-claim).** The publisher page was blocked to this session,
  including via a reader-proxy route, despite the paper's confirmed CC-BY open-access
  status (no independent PDF mirror was indexed anywhere at the time of this session);
  specific policy instruments, the precise trigger of the "developmental crisis," and the
  concrete before/after authority allocation between government tiers were not independently
  obtained.

## Bears On

- **Research:** [Xu: China's Land Finance as Active Mode](/wiki/xu-china-land-finance-active-mode/) — a state-versus-developer framing of Chinese land finance; this paper's intra-state, inter-governmental-tier framing is a distinct axis.
- **Concept:** [Land Value Capture](/wiki/land-value-capture/) — a case showing that even public capture of land rent raises live governance questions about which level of government captures it.
- **Research:** [Liu: Shanghai and Chongqing's Real Estate Tax Pilots](/wiki/liu-shanghai-chongqing-property-tax-pilots/) — a different Chinese fiscal instrument, useful comparison of tax-design vs. governance-design approaches to the same underlying land-finance system.
