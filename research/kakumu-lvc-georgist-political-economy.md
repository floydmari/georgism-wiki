## Summary

"The principles of land value capture in the perspective of Georgist political economy," by
**Owiti A. K'Akumu**, appeared in *Planning Perspectives* 40(2): 265–282 (2024). It is a
companion piece to the wiki's existing [K'Akumu: Kenya Site Value
Tax](/wiki/kakumu-kenya-site-value-tax/) page (a 2026 case study on colonial-era site-value-tax
diffusion) by the same author, but with a different, more foundational scope: a taxonomic
review of [land value capture](/wiki/land-value-capture/) as a general policy category.

## The Taxonomy

Per the paper's abstract, the article "tackles the question of unearned increment that
accrues to landowners in urban situations in terms of uplifts in land values" by
"undertaking a taxonomic examination of LVC strategies and instruments to end a fragmentary
understanding of the phenomenon." Its central move is to identify **Georgist and non-Georgist
typologies of unearned increment**, paying particular attention to the tax-based strategy of
LVC — [site value taxation](/wiki/site-value/), "mainly associated with Henry
George" — and drawing conceptual distinctions between this and **betterment**-family
instruments: planning-gain levies and special-assessment rates, which capture value at the
point of a specific development decision rather than through a standing, recurring tax on
land value generally.

## Relation to the Georgist Case

This directly strengthens the wiki's [land value capture](/wiki/land-value-capture/), [site
value](/wiki/site-value/), and [betterment levy](/wiki/betterment-levy/) pages, which
document many individual LVC instruments and case studies but had not previously carried a
single unifying taxonomy distinguishing the Georgist site-value-tax tradition from the
broader, non-Georgist betterment-capture family those other instruments belong to. It pairs
naturally with the wiki's existing [K'Akumu Kenya
case study](/wiki/kakumu-kenya-site-value-tax/), extending the same author's research program
from a specific national case into a general conceptual framework.

## Nuances and Limits

- **A taxonomic/theoretical review, not new empirical data.** The paper's contribution is
  conceptual clarification and categorization, not a new dataset or case study.
- **Abstract-level source (B-claim, upgraded from a fully verbatim abstract).** The publisher
