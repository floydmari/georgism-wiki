## Overview

"Progress and Poverty: Walter Rodney's Legacy" is a 2026 article by **Franklin Obeng-Odoom** in the *American Journal of Economics and Sociology*, vol. 85, issue 2, pp. 283–293, doi:[10.1111/ajes.70021](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajes.70021).[1] Obeng-Odoom is a Ghanaian-Australian political economist already cited on the wiki for his comparative treatment of George and Elinor Ostrom on the commons.[2] The article is part of AJES vol. 85, no. 2 (March 2026), a themed **"Special Issue: Henry George"** guest-introduced by Clifford W. Cobb's "Striving for a Harmonious Society: Leaders in the Georgist Tradition" — this wiki's [Xu, Huang & Li HCRS piece](/wiki/xu-huang-li-china-hcrs-george/) appears in the same issue.[3] Both Semantic Scholar and Crossref confirm this article carries a genuine CC-BY license (unlike the Xu/Huang/Li piece in the same issue, which Unpaywall records only as "bronze" — free-to-read, unlicensed). Despite the confirmed open license, the wiki was still unable to retrieve the full text on re-check (2026-08-10): Wiley's Cloudflare bot-detection blocked direct fetch, a browser-user-agent `curl` request, a text-extraction proxy, and the publisher's own CC-BY `pdfdirect` download link, all identically. This page remains built from the published abstract only; claims beyond it are marked `[VERIFY]`.

## The Argument (per abstract)

Obeng-Odoom's starting point is [Henry George](/wiki/henry-george/)'s "progress and poverty" paradox — that material progress and entrenched poverty rise together, which George attributed to "the private appropriation of socially created value."[1] The paper's contribution is that George "failed to consider race as a variable in the formation of the paradox," and it uses **Walter Rodney** (1942–1980), the Guyanese historian and author of *How Europe Underdeveloped Africa*, to supply that missing analysis.[1] Rodney's own scholarship, per the abstract, "demonstrated that the creation of property in labor during slavery was reproduced after emancipation in the production of landed property relations" — i.e., racialized property in *people* became racialized property in *land*.[1] The paper's specific claim is that socially produced value was captured not only by White in-groups but also by "non-Black in-groups (White and East Indian) who controlled land" — language that situates the analysis in Guyana's own post-emancipation racial-economic structure (Afro- and Indo-Guyanese), rather than the US-specific case the wiki's existing racial-wealth-gap narrative documents.[1]

The paper's normative conclusion is unusually direct for a Georgist-tradition venue: "**Neither socialism nor a Georgist single tax can remedy the situation**."[1] Its proposed remedy instead combines the two traditions the wiki's [reparations-vs-rent-capture analysis](/wiki/land-and-the-black-white-wealth-gap/) already treats as complements rather than substitutes: "an effective remedy for George's paradox can be found by combining Georgist taxation with a reparations program derived from the legacy of Walter Rodney," invoking a recent **International Court of Justice ruling that states are obliged to pay reparations for internationally wrongful acts attributed to them.**[1] (D-claim; interpretive/normative, attributed to the author — presented, not endorsed by the wiki.)

## Relation to the Wiki's Existing Coverage

This is new territory. The wiki's [Land and the Black–white wealth gap](/wiki/land-and-the-black-white-wealth-gap/) narrative already argues, independently, that rent capture addresses the land-rent channel only *going forward* and is not a substitute for reparations targeting a specific historical debt — reaching a similar structural conclusion (rent capture alone is insufficient) via US evidence (Darity & Mullen).[4] Obeng-Odoom's paper extends the same insufficiency argument to a different setting (Guyana/the wider post-colonial Caribbean, via Rodney) and grounds it explicitly in a **racialized theory of land appropriation** that includes a non-Black landholding "in-group" (East Indian) as well as White colonizers — a three-way racial-economic structure the wiki's existing US-focused narrative does not cover. It is best read as international corroboration, from within the Georgist scholarly tradition itself, that the single tax is not a sufficient racial remedy on its own.
