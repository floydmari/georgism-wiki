## Summary

"Overlapping generations models, multiplicity of steady states and momentary equilibria,
and economic fluctuations," by **[Tomohiro Hirano](/wiki/tomohiro-hirano/)** and
**[Joseph E. Stiglitz](/wiki/joseph-stiglitz/)**, published in the *Oxford Review of
Economic Policy* 41(2), 2025, pp. 260–290 (DOI:
[10.1093/oxrep/graf026](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxrep/graf026); also circulated as
[NBER Working Paper 34193](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34193)) — appearing in the same
Oxford Review issue as, and immediately preceding, [the program's flagship "Henry George"
paper](/wiki/oxford-review-george-2025/) (pp. 326–357). **This paper's model contains no
land.** It is the general mathematical-economics foundation for the entire [Hirano-Stiglitz
research program](/wiki/oxford-review-george-2025/): a proof, using **quite general
utility and production functions**, that even the simplest possible textbook OLG model can
exhibit multiple steady states, multiple momentary equilibria, and complex fluctuations —
establishing that the "wobbly dynamics" the program's land-specific papers document are
not an artifact of unusual modeling choices about land, but a structural possibility
present in the OLG framework itself under conditions the authors show are not exotic.

## The Three Results

- **First: multiple steady states are possible.** The simplest OLG model can have more
  than one steady-state equilibrium under general conditions.
- **Second: multiple momentary equilibria are possible under rational expectations.** At
  any given point in time, more than one equilibrium can be consistent with rational
  expectations — the mathematical precondition for the "phase transition" concept used
  throughout the program's land papers.
- **Third: complex, non-periodic dynamics follow, but within well-defined bounds.** One
  implication of multiple momentary equilibria is that the economy's dynamics can be
  marked by fluctuations that lack even regular periodicity — yet these fluctuations are
  not unbounded chaos; they stay within calculable ranges the authors' framework can
  characterize.

## Relation to the Georgist Case

This paper's role in the wiki is foundational rather than directly evidentiary: it is the
proof that makes the rest of the program's land-speculation results legitimate rather than
special pleading. If multiplicity and wobbling required land specifically, or some unusual
modeling trick, the land-speculation results would be a curiosity; this paper shows the
underlying mathematical possibility is present in "the simplest of OLG models" under
general conditions, which is what licenses the program's land-specific papers to treat
land as *channeling and amplifying* a general instability rather than *inventing* one.
Readers who want the land-specific economics should start with [the flagship Henry George
paper](/wiki/oxford-review-george-2025/) or [the wobbly-dynamics land
paper](/wiki/hirano-stiglitz-wobbly-dynamics-land-speculation/); this page exists so the
wiki's citation of those papers can point to the general theorem underneath them.

## Nuances and Limits

- **No land in this paper.** Included in the wiki's Hirano-Stiglitz coverage for
  completeness and because it is the direct theoretical foundation the land papers build
  on, not because it makes a land-specific claim itself.
- **Abstract-level scan.** Both the NBER PDF and the Oxford Academic PDF were blocked
  (403/Cloudflare challenge) on every fetch method tried this session; this page is built
  from the published abstract and search-result characterizations of the paper's three
  results.
- **Highly technical, general-equilibrium theory.** This is pure mathematical economics;
  it makes no empirical or policy claim on its own.
