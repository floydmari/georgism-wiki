## Summary

"Agrarian Platform Capitalism: Digital Rentiership Comes to Farming," by **Emily Reisman,
Madeleine Fairbairn (UC Santa Cruz), and Zenia Kish**, appeared in *Antipode* 57: 412–432
(2025). It extends the wiki's [platform and data rents](/wiki/data-rents/) coverage into a
domain not previously documented: agriculture, where digital platforms are reshaping how
farmland itself, not just agricultural inputs, generates rent.

## Three Platform Categories

The paper identifies three overlapping types of agrarian platform: **farm management and
data platforms** (input e-commerce bundled with data aggregation, built out by major
agrochemical firms **Bayer, Syngenta, BASF, and Corteva**); **farmland real estate
platforms** (matching investors, landowners, and renters — **AcreValue, CommonGround
(formerly CashRent), CamoAg (formerly Tillable), AcreTrader, FarmFundr, FarmTogether**); and
**supply chain and market platforms** (food-safety traceability and commodity trading — **IBM
Food Trust**, a blockchain traceability system operating under the FDA's "New Era of Smarter
Food Safety" strategy, and **Covantis** for grain trading), plus carbon-market platforms
(**Indigo**, and **Nori**, which issues carbon-credit NFTs the paper describes as a "pseudo
currency").

## The Farmer's Business Network Case

The paper's central case study is **Farmer's Business Network (FBN)**, founded in 2014 on
explicitly anti-corporate-concentration messaging — its early value proposition revealed that
farmers were paying more for seed regionally than they should. By 2021, FBN had partnered
with grain giant **ADM**, which the authors read as a demonstration that "populist framing
obscures growing corporate power." FBN's actual business model, per the paper, is "owning the
digital real estate" — platform-fee extraction as the primary logic, with agricultural
product sales as secondary revenue.

## The Digital Rentiership Mechanism

The paper identifies five components: monopoly-style platform fees functioning as
unavoidable intermediaries with strong network-effect barriers to entry; remote/absentee
farmland ownership at unprecedented scale enabled by real-estate platforms; farmland
"crowdfunding" converting land into a genuine financial asset class, structurally similar to
the securitization logic documented elsewhere in the wiki's [rentier](/wiki/rentier/)
coverage; quasi-regulatory capture, where platforms (like IBM Food Trust) become embedded
within, rather than opposed to, government oversight; and populist anti-corporate rhetoric
that, per the paper, in practice "reproduce[s] concentrated power by a new name."

## Relation to the Georgist Case

This is a genuinely new domain for the wiki's [data rents](/wiki/data-rents/) coverage,
combining two distinct rent-extraction channels already documented separately elsewhere on
the wiki — platform/data rents and land financialization — applied specifically to
agricultural land. The farmland-crowdfunding mechanism in particular extends the wiki's
[financialization of land](/wiki/financialization-of-land/) coverage into a sector (farmland)
not previously examined, while the FBN case is a concrete, well-documented instance of
platform rhetoric obscuring rather than resisting rent extraction.

## Nuances and Limits

- **A conceptual/synthesis paper built on named case examples**, not a quantitative study
  measuring the scale of agrarian platform rent extraction.
- **US-centric case selection** (Bayer/Syngenta/BASF/Corteva, FBN/ADM, IBM Food Trust/FDA are
  all primarily US-market examples); international generalizability is not established here.
- **Abstract-level and secondary-source-level (B-claim).** The publisher page was blocked to
  this session; this page is built from a detailed author-published plain-language summary
  of the paper (an "abbreviated version... originally published in Antipode," per the source
  itself), not the peer-reviewed article's own full text, which has no open-access copy
  anywhere per Unpaywall.
