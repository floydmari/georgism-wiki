## Summary

"Assessing the Economic Potential of Public Goods in Azerbaijan," a working paper by **Nihad
Isgandarov and Ilkin Mustafayev**, posted on Cambridge Open Engage in August 2026, examines
[Azerbaijan](/wiki/azerbaijan/)'s "paradox of plenty" — the recurring pattern in which
resource-rich nations fail to convert natural-resource revenue into robust public services —
through the specific lens of the country's public-goods provision (transport, infrastructure,
environmental management) over 2000-2024. It is the wiki's first dedicated case study of the
Caucasus/Caspian region's resource-rent economies.

## Fiscal Capacity Is Not the Binding Constraint

The paper's central finding inverts the most common assumption about resource-cursed
economies: Azerbaijan's **State Oil Fund (SOFAZ)**, its oil-and-gas sovereign wealth fund,
provides substantial financial resources — fiscal capacity is not, on the authors' analysis,
the primary obstacle to better public-goods outcomes. Instead, successful delivery depends more
on "the quality of public investment management, the credibility of fiscal rules, and the
discipline of project selection" than on spending volume itself. This is a specific,
institutional-quality diagnosis rather than a generic "more oversight needed" claim.

## Transboundary and Infrastructure Findings

Two further findings round out the paper's descriptive-analysis methodology (a structured
literature review combined with macro-fiscal, infrastructural, and environmental indicators
spanning 2000-2024). First, Caspian Sea environmental management and shared water systems
require sustained regional cooperation because they cross borders — a constraint fiscal
capacity alone cannot solve. Second, transport, reconstruction, and digital-infrastructure
investment yields significant social returns only when paired with transparent evaluation
processes and sustained maintenance funding — i.e., capital spending without an accompanying
institutional-quality investment tends to underperform its potential.

## Relation to the Georgist Case

This is a useful complement to the wiki's [sovereign wealth fund](/wiki/sovereign-wealth-fund/)
coverage, which generally treats a well-run fund (Norway, Alaska) as evidence that resource
rents *can* be captured and distributed effectively. Azerbaijan's case, on the authors' own
telling, is not a story of failed *capture* — SOFAZ exists and holds substantial assets — but a
story of what happens **after** capture: converting captured rent into genuine public value
requires the same institutional-quality preconditions (credible fiscal rules, disciplined
project selection, transparent evaluation) that the wiki's assessment-and-administrative-capacity
coverage documents for land value taxation specifically. A resource-rent fund is a necessary
but not sufficient condition for the "paradox of plenty" to be solved.

## Nuances and Limits

- **Working-paper tier, not peer-reviewed.** Posted to Cambridge Open Engage's preprint
  platform; this page treats it as Supplementary tier pending a published or peer-reviewed
  version.
- **Descriptive rather than causal.** The methodology (literature review plus descriptive
  indicator analysis) supports a diagnosis of *what correlates with* better outcomes, not a
  causal estimate of institutional-quality reforms' effect size.
- **B-claim.** Read via a fetched summary of the paper rather than the full underlying text;
  specific figures and the complete indicator set were not independently extracted this
  session.
