## Summary

"Private Land Ownership: Tax or Socialize?" by **Dirk Loehr** appeared in the *American
Journal of Economics and Sociology* in 2026 (DOI 10.1111/ajes.70033), the same year, journal,
and author as the wiki's existing page on Loehr's [Henry George and Silvio Gesell: The Odd
Couple](/wiki/loehr-george-gesell-odd-couple/) (DOI 10.1111/ajes.70039). The two are
companion pieces sharing a premise — that George and Gesell both "rejected private
appropriation of land rent as unjust" — but this paper narrows the "Odd Couple" comparison's
broad money-land-taxation sweep down to one concrete, applied policy question: should private
land ownership be **taxed** (George's route) or **socialized** (Gesell's route)?

## The Argument

Per the paper's abstract, George proposed to "hollow out" private land ownership "through a
comprehensive [land value tax](/wiki/land-value-tax/)," leaving legal title in private hands
but capturing the economic rent through taxation. [Silvio Gesell](/wiki/silvio-gesell/), by
contrast, "aimed at full socialization of land combined with lease auctions and compensation
of former owners through interest-bearing government bonds, embedded within his free money
system" — public ownership from the outset, with existing owners bought out rather than
taxed indefinitely. Loehr's own conclusion, quoted from the abstract: "a forward-looking land
regime should combine elements of both thinkers — Gesell's socialization principles for
high-value urban land and George's land value taxation for peripheral areas."

## Relation to the Georgist Case

This is a genuine sharpening, not a restatement, of the "Odd Couple" comparison: rather than
leaving George's and Gesell's land programs as parallel-but-different reform traditions, this
paper stakes out a specific spatial hybrid — nationalize (via buyout and auction) the highest-
value urban land where the case for public capture is strongest and administratively
simplest, while taxing peripheral and rural land where full socialization would be harder to
justify or administer. It is a useful counterpoint to full land nationalization as an alternative to LVT: Loehr's
hybrid treats the tax-versus-socialize choice as a matter of degree and location rather than
an all-or-nothing doctrinal commitment.

## Nuances and Limits

- **Genuinely distinct from, but closely paired with, the "Odd Couple" paper.** Same author,
  journal, and year; readers interested in the fuller George/Gesell theoretical comparison
  (money, interest, negative-rate debates) should read [Loehr's companion
  page](/wiki/loehr-george-gesell-odd-couple/) alongside this one.
  Both papers should be read as a pair rather than either substituting for the other.
- **Abstract-level source (B-claim).** Full text was not accessible to this session (Wiley
  paywall/bot-detection, consistent with the wiki's other AJES-page retrieval failures);
  this page is built from the published abstract, independently corroborated across multiple
  search results, not the paper's full argument or its treatment of implementation detail
  (how the buyout bonds would be priced, how the urban/peripheral line would be drawn).
- **The "high-value urban vs. peripheral" line is the author's own proposed division**,
  not a standard categorization elsewhere in the land-value-capture literature the wiki cites.
