## Overview

"Did Henry George Inspire China's Economic Miracle?" is a 2026 article by **Wei Xu, Yi Huang, and Ling Li** in the *American Journal of Economics and Sociology*, doi:[10.1111/ajes.70013](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajes.70013).[1] Full text was not retrievable — Wiley blocks automated fetching regardless of the article's access status, and neither Unpaywall nor Semantic Scholar confirmed an open-access copy at the time of writing. This page is built from the published abstract only; claims beyond it are marked [VERIFY].

## The Argument (per abstract)

The paper's subject is the **Household Contract Responsibility System (HCRS)**, the policy that — under Deng Xiaoping's "socialism with Chinese characteristics" — launched China's post-1978 growth: land remains state-owned, but farmers gained "the right to autonomous production and disposal of surplus products," sharply improving agricultural output and incomes.[1] The authors argue this arrangement "bears a striking resemblance" to [Henry George](/wiki/henry-george/)'s program: under George's system land is nominally private, but "the full economic value of land is captured by the state" through a tax, so that "the net effect is almost exactly the same as a system of state-owned land" — and in both systems, workers retain the full value of their labor once their social obligation (rent or tax) is met.[1] The authors' own framing is explicitly *structural, not historical*: "Henry George did not inspire China's economic miracle, but the basic principles are the same."[1] The paper's rhetorical edge is aimed at the West, contrasting China's adoption of this land-labor logic (three decades of rapid growth) with Western economies that "continue to ignore Henry George's ideas."[1] (D/C-claim; a theoretical/interpretive analogy, not a claim of causal historical influence — the authors state this distinction themselves.)

## Relation to the Wiki's Existing China Coverage

This is a different topic from the wiki's existing China material. [Gaffney's 2015 "Real-Assets Model" paper](/wiki/gaffney-real-assets-model-china/) addresses China's property-driven business cycle and predicted the 2015 downturn — a macro-financial argument unrelated to land tenure design. [Sun Yat-sen](/wiki/sun-yat-sen/)'s page documents a direct, historically-attested transmission of George's ideas into Chinese land policy (Sun explicitly cited George), which fed into Taiwan's land value increment tax. The HCRS argument is different in kind from both: it is a **structural-convergence** claim about Mao-era collectivized land plus Deng-era household contracting, argued to functionally resemble Georgist land-value capture — with the authors explicitly disclaiming any historical lineage from George to Deng Xiaoping's reforms. No prior wiki page draws this analogy.

## Standing and Limits

- **Abstract-only scan; the argument is analogical, not historical.** [VERIFY: full text unread — the depth of the analogy, and any qualifications the authors attach to it, cannot be assessed from the abstract.] The claim that HCRS's "net effect is almost exactly the same as a system of state-owned land" is a strong theoretical equivalence claim that a careful reading might qualify (e.g., differences in how rent/tax rates are set, tenure security, transferability).
- **Not evidence for a causal Georgist mechanism in China's growth.** The authors are explicit that they are not claiming George influenced Chinese policy; readers should not cite this paper as evidence that Georgist theory *caused* China's growth, only as an argument that HCRS's structure is analogous to Georgist land-value capture.
