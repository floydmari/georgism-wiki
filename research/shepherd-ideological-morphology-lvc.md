## Summary

"Public policy and contested political concepts: the ideological morphology of land value
capture," by **Edward Shepherd** (Cardiff University), appeared in the *Journal of Political
Ideologies*, March 2025, as CC-BY open access. It applies Michael Freeden's morphological
method — normally used to analyze whole political ideologies like liberalism or conservatism —
to a single contested policy area: [land value capture](/wiki/land-value-capture/) in the UK,
via systematic coding of over 100 parliamentary debates and policy documents from 1941 to
2023.

## The Morphology

Freeden's method distinguishes **core concepts** (without which a debate has no coherent
meaning) from **adjacent concepts** (which give the core specificity). Shepherd identifies
land value, community, property, and the role of the state as core to LVC debate, with the
**landowner** also core but unusually unstable — since it is embodied in real, heterogeneous
people (speculator, hereditary steward, farmer, developer, ordinary homeowner) rather than a
clean abstraction. Democracy and liberty sit as adjacent concepts, legitimizing or
constraining the state's claim to act on behalf of the community.

The paper's central dynamic finding is that **the market migrated from an adjacent to a core
concept over time**. In the first policy period (1940s–1985), Labour sought to expel the
market from land allocation entirely — land value was read as community-created (echoing
Ricardo, Mill, George, and the Fabians), landowners were framed as passive rentiers "who sit
there and do nothing" (Gilbert Mitchison, 1952), and 1966/1974/1979 manifestos backed
compulsory purchase of development land at existing-use value. Conservatives countered by
invoking **liberty** and rejecting the community-equals-state conflation — "the community,
according to the Government, is the local authorities, although many would challenge that
statement" (Baroness Young, 1975) — and championed a wide "property-owning democracy" over
state land ownership.

In the second period (1985–present), both parties converged on treating the market as the
natural price mechanism, narrowing the state's role to a passive regulator releasing land
supply rather than an active landowner. Shepherd explicitly connects this shift to Clark's
displacement of classical land theory — citing Gaffney's characterization of it as "an
orchestrated campaign of academic terrorism" against George (via Gaffney & Harrison's *The
Corruption of Economics*), the same historical claim the wiki's [Charlier &
Tideman](/wiki/charlier-tideman-clarks-paradigm/) page documents in much greater depth.

## The Conceptual Silence Around Homeowners

A particularly sharp finding: both parties could jointly condemn the "speculative owner of
development land" as illegitimate — but homeowners, who constitute roughly 65% of English
households and are equally beneficiaries of land value appreciation, were left what Freeden
calls a **"conceptual silence"** — never subjected to the same moral critique, because taxing
ordinary homeowner land wealth was politically toxic to both parties. Shepherd also finds a
partial third-period return to active-state framing: Labour's 2017 and 2019 manifestos
pledged an LVT, and the Conservative government's 2023 legislation enabling below-market-value
compulsory purchase (net of "hope value") is read as an unexpected right-of-center revival of
active-landowner-state logic.

## Relation to the Georgist Case

This gives the wiki's [land value capture](/wiki/land-value-capture/) and [betterment
levy](/wiki/betterment-levy/) pages, which document the UK's repeated cycle of enacting and
repealing LVC instruments, a political-theory account of *why* that cycle happens: the
underlying concepts LVC depends on (land value as community-created, the landowner's moral
standing, the market's proper role) are genuinely and persistently contested rather than
settled, and the specific line drawn between "illegitimate speculator" and "ordinary
homeowner" — never between all land value gains and none — is itself a political choice with
a traceable history, not a natural boundary.

## Nuances and Limits

- **UK-specific, parliamentary-debate-based.** The method and findings are grounded in
  Westminster political discourse specifically; the "conceptual silence" around
  homeowners may not generalize identically to other countries' political systems.
- **A discourse-analytic study, not a policy-effectiveness study.** The paper documents how
  LVC has been argued about, not whether any given LVC instrument works.
- **Full text read directly (A-claim).** The complete article and its 110 endnotes were
  obtained (via a reader-proxy route after direct fetches to both the publisher and Cardiff's
  ORCA repository were blocked) and read in full.
