## Overview

The **Coalition for Economic Justice (C4EJ)** is a British cross-party advocacy group,
comprising "political groups, pressure groups and charities that came together in 2008 in
response to the economic crisis of that year," proposing "the introduction of an annual
Location Value Tax (LVT) (also known as a Land Value Tax) to reduce existing taxes on
enterprise and labour."[1] Its steering group meets monthly at 11 Mandeville Place, London — the
[School of Economic Science](/wiki/school-of-economic-science/)'s London headquarters — and
draws contributors from across the UK's Georgist and land-reform organizations, including
[Andrew Purves](/wiki/andrew-purves/) and members of the [Labour Land
Campaign](/wiki/labour-land-campaign/).[1][2]

## History and Leadership

C4EJ convened House of Commons seminars promoting LVT in March 2009 and again in March
2016.[1] John Lipetz chaired the coalition for its first eight years before stepping down in
March 2016; Peter Bowman succeeded him as Chair in April 2016, with Ed Randall and David Hirst
as Deputy Chairs and Rob Blakemore continuing as Secretary.[2] New steering-group members
joining in 2016-17 included Andrew Purves (affiliated with the School of Economic Science),
Peter Smith (Labour Land Campaign), and economist Laurie Heykoop.[2]

## The OPDC Submission

In a joint submission to London's New London Plan public consultation, C4EJ's Working Group on
the Old Oak and Park Royal Development Corporation (OPDC) — authored by Ed Randall on behalf of
the group, with Andrew Purves and John Lipetz credited as contributors — argued for capturing
land-value uplift to fund the OPDC's redevelopment of one of London's largest regeneration
sites.[1] The submission proposed an irrevocable OPDC land trust, partnerships with public
developers modeled on Hong Kong's [MTR Corporation](/wiki/mtr-corporation/) and London &
Continental Railways, Public Works Loan Board funding, and ground rents tied to land value,
citing a companion report titled "Let's Supercharge the OPDC and bring 'Singapore' to Thames
side."[1] It cited UK Budget "Blue Book" data (via the Office for National Statistics) showing
UK land value rose from £0.7 trillion to £3.9 trillion (+479%) between 1996 and 2016, versus
dwelling value growth of only £0.5 trillion to £1.5 trillion (+203%) over the same period, with
land coming to exceed 70% of residential property value.[1]

## 2016-17 Activity

C4EJ's own annual report for 2016-17 documents a wide and specific range of engagement.
Following the Greater London Authority Planning Committee's February 2016 report "Tax Trial: A
Land Value Tax for London?", then-Mayor Sadiq Khan responded positively in January 2017,
expressing interest in an LVT pilot through the OPDC though noting primary legislation would be
required; the London Finance Committee's "Devolution: a capital idea" (January 2017)
recommended trialing an LVT pilot on undeveloped land, and a GLA/TfL "Land Value Capture" report
(February 2017) set out a ten-point plan to improve land-value-uplift extraction.[2] At
Westminster, a House of Commons seminar chaired by Kelvin Hopkins MP (23 March 2016) featured
Stuart Adam (Institute for Fiscal Studies), Heather Wetzel (Labour Land Campaign), and Molly
Scott Cato MEP, with Shadow Chancellor John McDonnell in attendance and voicing support,
although sustained follow-up engagement with his policy team proved slow.[2] In Scotland, C4EJ
tracked the Scottish Land Reform Bill's Royal Assent (April 2016) and the founding of the
Scottish Land Commission (April 2017), and reported the March 2017 SNP Conference passing a
motion committing the Scottish government to explore "all fiscal options including ways of
taxing the value of undeveloped land."[2] The report also references the [New Economics
Foundation](/wiki/new-economics-foundation/)'s "Rethinking the Economics of Land & Housing"
(February 2017, by Josh Ryan-Collins, Toby Lloyd, and Laurie Macfarlane), [Dave
Wetzel](/wiki/dave-wetzel/)'s "Underground Treasure" report on LVT for rail funding (commissioned
by the RMT union, September 2016), and David Triggs — President of the [International Union for
Land Value Taxation](/wiki/international-union-lvt/) — attending the UN's Habitat III conference
in Quito (October 2016), whose resulting New Urban Agenda endorsed land-value-capture measures
in its Section 137.[2]

## Relation to the Georgist Case

C4EJ is a rare, documented example of a durable, cross-party UK advocacy coalition sustaining
land-value-tax engagement with Parliament, the London Assembly, and the Scottish government
across more than a decade — a useful complement to the wiki's coverage of the [Labour Land
Campaign](/wiki/labour-land-campaign/) and the [Henry George Foundation
(UK)](/wiki/henry-george-foundation-uk/), showing the institutional density of the UK's Georgist
movement beyond any single organization.
