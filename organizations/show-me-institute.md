## Overview

The **Show-Me Institute** is a free-market think tank founded in 2005 in Clayton, Missouri, by Rex Sinquefield, Crosby Kemper III, and economist Michael Podgursky, affiliated with the national State Policy Network of similar organizations.[1] Its stated mission is "improving the quality of life for all citizens of Missouri by advancing sensible, well-researched solutions to state and local policy issues," with a general orientation toward lower taxes, smaller government, and deregulation.[1] Its very first publication (2006), by University of Missouri economist Joseph Haslag, argued that the 1% municipal **earnings taxes** levied by Kansas City and St. Louis were suppressing personal income growth in those cities relative to their untaxed suburbs.[1]

## Land Value Tax Advocacy

Unusually for a free-market institute, the Show-Me Institute's proposed alternative to the earnings tax has consistently been **expanded land value taxation** rather than a cut in overall taxation or a shift to sales taxes. In a series of articles from 2010 to 2026, its writers — Christine Harbin, David Stokes, and Joseph Haslag among them — argued that Kansas City's existing land-only **Parkway** and **Trafficway Maintenance Taxes** (raising roughly $9.9 million/year as of 2012) should be *expanded*, not eliminated as a 2012 city revenue commission proposed, and that both Kansas City and St. Louis should shift toward land taxation to replace their earnings taxes, citing Henry George, Milton Friedman, Paul Samuelson, and Joseph Stiglitz as cross-ideological economists who favor land taxation on efficiency grounds.[2] See [United States](/wiki/united-states/) for the fuller account of this advocacy record and its outcomes.
